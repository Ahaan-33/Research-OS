## Purpose

_Discovery Roadmap §6_ rejected point-set topology for the semantic map as overreach ("nothing in the architecture needs open sets, continuity, or a metric — a poset of coarsenings says everything that's actually being claimed") and named the correct object as a **filtration/coarsening poset**. _[[08-Semantic-Distance-and-Meaning]]_ then showed why: the underlying proximity structure is a pseudo-semimetric at best (T4), which is exactly the condition under which classical point-set topology (built from open balls in a metric) cannot be honestly claimed.

This document performs the Fifth Task — "the architecture repeatedly states topology emerges from semantics. Now prove it" — by taking that rejection seriously rather than working around it, and asks the sharper question: _is there a genuine topology (in the precise mathematical sense — a family of open sets satisfying the topology axioms) that emerges from the coarsening poset, without needing a metric at all?_

The answer is yes, and it resolves what could otherwise look like a tension between "topology emerges from semantics" (the governing philosophical claim) and "no point-set topology" (the prior formal finding): **the coarsening poset itself generates a genuine topology — an Alexandrov topology — satisfying real topological axioms, requiring no metric, no continuity in the classical sense, and no open balls.** This document proves that, and only that; it does not reverse _Discovery Roadmap_'s rejection, it sharpens it.

References back to: _Discovery Roadmap §6_, _[[06-Research-State-Mathematics]]_ (the poset `(Legal, ⊑)`), _[[08-Semantic-Distance-and-Meaning]]_ (D18–D20, T6, T7 — the abstraction poset and the neighbourhood/cluster distinction this document builds directly on).

## Definitions

### D21 — Cluster (Connected Component)

Fix a threshold `θ`. Define the **proximity graph** `G_θ = (Current(E), Edges_θ)` with `(e,e') ∈ Edges_θ` iff `proximity(e,e') ≥ θ` (_[[08-Semantic-Distance-and-Meaning]]_, D16). A **cluster** at threshold `θ` is a connected component of `G_θ`. Per T7, this is the transitive closure of the (possibly non-transitive) neighbourhood relation, and is therefore always well-defined as an equivalence relation on `Current(E)`, regardless of whether `proximity` itself satisfies any metric property.

### D22 — Region

A **region** at abstraction level `a ∈ Abs` (_[[08-Semantic-Distance-and-Meaning]]_, D18) is simply one group of the partition `a`. Regions at the finest level are singleton clusters (individual elements); regions at coarser levels are unions of finer regions, by the definition of `≼`.

### D23 — Alexandrov Topology on the Coarsening Poset

Recall a standard fact from general topology: **every preorder `(X, ≤)` induces an Alexandrov topology** on `X`, whose open sets are exactly the **down-sets** (or, dually, up-sets) of the preorder — sets `U` such that `x ∈ U` and `y ≤ x` implies `y ∈ U`. In an Alexandrov space, arbitrary intersections of open sets are open (not merely finite intersections, as in general topology) — this is the defining, stronger property of Alexandrov spaces, and it is exactly the property that makes "generated by a preorder" and "a genuine topology" coincide.

Apply this directly to `(Abs, ≼)` (_[[08-Semantic-Distance-and-Meaning]]_, T6): define

```
Open(U) :⟺ U ⊆ Abs is down-closed under ≼
```

**Theorem T8 (Genuine Topology on Abstraction Levels).** `(Abs, τ_Alexandrov)`, where `τ_Alexandrov` is the set of all `≼`-down-closed subsets of `Abs`, is a genuine topological space: `∅` and `Abs` are open; arbitrary unions of opens are open; **arbitrary intersections of opens are open** (the Alexandrov-specific strengthening). _Proof._ Standard — down-closure is preserved under arbitrary unions and intersections of down-closed sets, by definition of down-closure applied pointwise. ∎

**This is the proof the task requested.** "Topology emerges from semantics" is true, and precisely so: `Abs` itself — the set of ways the _same_ Research State can be viewed at different resolutions, ordered by refinement (_[[08-Semantic-Distance-and-Meaning]]_, D18/D19, itself built from `I`'s coordinates, i.e. from semantics) — carries a bona fide topology whose open sets are exactly the down-closed sets of abstraction levels. No metric was used; no open ball was constructed; the topology _is_ the coarsening poset, viewed correctly.

### D24 — Minimal Neighbourhood of a Point

In any Alexandrov space, each point `x` has a **minimal open neighbourhood** `↓x := \{ y : y ≤ x \}$` (the down-set generated by `x` alone) — the smallest open set containing `x`. For `a ∈ Abs`: `↓a = \{ a' \in Abs : a' \preceq a \}$` — every abstraction level at least as coarse as `a`. This gives "neighbourhood" (as the task asks for) a precise, non-metric meaning distinct from _[[08-Semantic-Distance-and-Meaning]]_'s `N_θ(e)` (a neighbourhood of an _element_ in proximity space) — D24 is a neighbourhood of an _abstraction level_ in the coarsening space. Both are legitimately "neighbourhoods"; they live in different spaces (`Current(E)` under proximity, versus `Abs` under coarsening) and should not be conflated.

## Connected Components, Boundaries, Bridges

These notions are defined on the **proximity graph** `G_θ` (D21), at a fixed abstraction level, and are standard graph-theoretic notions, imported directly (per the Seventh Task's instruction to use existing mathematics where it already fits) rather than requiring new machinery:

- **Connected component:** as D21 — a maximal cluster.
- **Boundary** of a region `R` (a cluster or coarser aggregate, D22): the set of elements `e ∈ R` with at least one edge in `Edges_θ` to an element `e' ∉ R`. An element with no such edge is **interior** to `R`.
- **Bridge:** an edge `(e,e') ∈ Edges_θ` whose removal disconnects a cluster into two or more components — a single-point-of-failure connection between two otherwise-separate regions. Scientifically, a bridge element pair is exactly a "the one piece of evidence linking two lines of investigation" case — losing (superseding without adequate replacement interpretation, per Ambiguity Audit A2) either side of a bridge is a specific, formally identifiable risk to a project's connectivity, and the Projection Layer could reasonably flag bridges as structurally significant for exactly this reason.

**Theorem T9 (Bridges Are Threshold-Relative).** Whether an edge is a bridge depends on `θ`: raising `θ` (demanding higher proximity to count as connected) can only ever _remove_ edges from `Edges_θ`, never add them, so the _set_ of bridges is itself a function of `θ`, generally non-monotone in an obvious direction (removing a non-bridge edge can create a bridge elsewhere; removing a bridge edge disconnects the graph, at which point "bridge" for the remaining edges must be recomputed per-component). _Proof._ Direct from `Edges_θ ⊇ Edges_{θ'}$` for `θ ≤ θ'` and the standard graph-theoretic definition of bridges. ∎ This theorem is the formal reason Projection's Abstraction Parameter (which effectively selects `θ`, or an equivalent coarsening criterion) genuinely changes which structural features (bridges, in particular) are visible — not merely how much detail is shown, but which _relationships_ are structurally load-bearing at all.

## Basins and Flows

The task asks for "basins" and "flows" — dynamical-systems vocabulary. Per the Seventh Task's caution ("do not adopt any framework because it is fashionable... use them only if they naturally explain the architecture already present"), this document translates rather than imports: the only genuine dynamics in the architecture is **Synthesize's repeated application relative to a fixed `E`** (_[[07-Transformation-Algebra]]_, G4) — there is no other process in the system that evolves a state through a sequence of steps toward some outcome. So "flow" and "basin" are given meaning _only_ in terms of that one process, and nowhere else — this document does not manufacture a general-purpose dynamical system where none exists.

### D25 — Flow

The **flow** generated by Synthesize (fixed `E`) is the sequence `I₀, I₁ = Synthesize(E,I₀), I₂ = Synthesize(E,I₁), …` — i.e., iterating G4. Per _[[07-Transformation-Algebra]]_, this sequence is monotone non-decreasing under `⊑` unconditionally, and (conditionally, pending the open idempotence question) eventually constant.

### D26 — Basin of Attraction

For a closed point `I^*` (a complete state relative to `E`, _[[06-Research-State-Mathematics]]_, "Completion"), its **basin** is:

```
Basin(I^*) := { I₀ : the flow from I₀ eventually reaches I^* }
```

**Theorem T10 (Basins Partition Reachable States, Conditionally).** _If_ Synthesize is idempotent (the still-open question from _[[07-Transformation-Algebra]]_), _and if_ each starting `I₀` converges to a unique closed point (a further, separate, also-unproven claim — uniqueness of the limit is not automatic even given idempotence, since two different starting `I₀`'s could plausibly converge to two different closed points, which is not a defect, only a fact to note), _then_ the basins of distinct closed points partition the reachable interpretation space for fixed `E`. This theorem is stated with its two hypotheses explicit precisely because neither is proven — this document does not claim more than the algebra currently supports, consistent with the task's instruction not to force elegance.

### D27 — Singularity

A **singularity** is a legal state `(E,I)` at which the flow (D25) does **not** converge to a unique closed point reachable by continuing to apply Synthesize alone — i.e., a Conflict Region (`|I(e,d)| ≥ 2`, per _[[06-Research-State-Mathematics]]_, "Contradiction") that **cannot be closed by any amount of re-running Synthesize against the same, unchanged `E`** — it requires new Evidence (a further Capture) to break the impasse. This gives "singularity" a precise, checkable meaning: it is exactly a **structurally unresolvable Conflict Region relative to current Evidence** — the formal counterpart of _Scientific Synthesis_'s framing of contradiction as something that sometimes genuinely requires new observation, not just more analysis of what's already known.

**This is not a new phenomenon requiring new machinery** — it is precisely the case where `Synthesize(E, ·)`, iterated, reaches a fixed point that is _not_ a closed point in the Completion sense (D26's target `I^*` still contains an open Conflict Region, and repeating Synthesize changes nothing further). The "singularity" vocabulary is retained here only because it names a case worth distinguishing operationally (Publication tooling should treat "converged but still conflicted" differently from "still converging"), not because it requires any structure beyond what G4 already defines.

## Conflict Regions, Revisited Topologically

_[[06-Research-State-Mathematics]]_ defined `Conflicts(S)` as a derived predicate over `I`. This document adds: a Conflict Region, viewed as a subset of `Current(E)` (the elements whose coordinates are in tension), is generally **not** a single cluster (D21) — it can span multiple clusters if the contradiction connects organisationally distant elements (per _[[08-Semantic-Distance-and-Meaning]]_, T5's independence finding: contradiction and proximity don't determine each other). This means Conflict Regions are best modeled not as regions in the `Abs`/cluster sense (D22) at all, but as their own structure — a **hypergraph** over `E × D` where each hyperedge is the set of elements jointly implicated by one open conflict. This is consistent with, and gives precise shape to, _Discovery Roadmap §6_'s flagged-but-unformalized "conflict regions" item in its topology wishlist — the answer is: Conflict Regions are a hypergraph structure layered _on top of_ the cluster/region topology, not a special case _within_ it.

## Does the Resulting Structure Satisfy Known Mathematical Properties?

Directly addressing the task's explicit question:

- **`(Abs, τ_Alexandrov)` is a genuine topological space** (T8), and moreover a genuine **Alexandrov space**, a well-studied, named structure in classical general topology (equivalent to preordered sets; see _[[11-Formal-Foundations-Survey]]_). No new mathematics was needed here — this is existing mathematics correctly identified and applied, per the Seventh Task's discipline.
- **The proximity graph `G_θ` (D21) is a standard weighted graph**, and clusters, boundaries, and bridges are entirely standard graph-theoretic notions requiring no extension.
- **Basins and flows (D25–D27), conditionally on unresolved idempotence,** would form a standard discrete dynamical system on a poset — but this remains conditional, not proven, and this document is explicit that it should not be oversold as more settled than it is.
- **Conflict Regions as hypergraphs** is a standard structure (hypergraphs are well-studied), applied here rather than invented.

**Nothing in this document requires inventing new mathematics.** Every structure named by the task (neighbourhoods, connected components, regions, boundaries, bridges, clusters, singularities, conflict regions, basins, flows) is shown above to be an instance of an existing, named mathematical object — Alexandrov topology, graph connectivity, discrete dynamical systems on a poset, hypergraphs — correctly matched to the corresponding architectural concept. This is the intended outcome per the task's own instruction: "if existing mathematics is insufficient, explicitly describe where the project extends beyond it" — the finding here is that existing mathematics is, in fact, sufficient, once matched carefully.

## Engineering Implications

- **The Projection Layer's Abstraction Parameter should be understood, and can be implemented, as literally selecting a point in the Alexandrov space `(Abs, τ_Alexandrov)`** — "zooming out" is moving to a coarser point in the poset, and its minimal neighbourhood (D24) is precisely "everything at least this coarse," giving "zoom out reveals a well-defined, structured set of coarser views" a literal, not metaphorical, meaning.
- **Bridge-detection (T9) is a concrete, implementable feature** for the Relationship/Emergence Engine or Projection Layer: flagging bridge elements/relations as structurally significant is a standard graph algorithm (bridge-finding via DFS low-link values), directly implementable against the derived proximity graph `G_θ` at whatever threshold the current view uses.
- **Conflict Regions should be stored and queried as a hypergraph structure distinct from the cluster/graph structure** — this is a concrete data-modeling consequence: a Conflict Region's "spread" across potentially many, potentially organisationally-distant elements means it cannot be efficiently queried merely by asking "what's near this element" (D16 proximity) — it needs its own index, keyed on the hyperedge structure directly.
- **"Singularity" (D27) gives Publication tooling a precise, distinguishable state to detect and report**: "this region has converged under repeated Synthesis but remains conflicted" is a different, actionable message to a researcher than "still processing," and is now formally well-defined enough to implement as a distinct status.

## Rejected Alternatives

- **Constructing a metric topology (open balls under `dist` from _[[08-Semantic-Distance-and-Meaning]]_).** Rejected, consistent with _Discovery Roadmap §6_'s original finding and reinforced by T4's pseudo-semimetric result: since `dist` isn't even a proper metric, a ball-based topology built from it would be ill-founded (open balls under a non-definite, non-triangle-respecting "distance" don't reliably generate a sensible topology without extensive further conditions).
- **Persistent homology / full topological data analysis machinery.** Considered for "basins" and "singularities," given TDA's native vocabulary of features appearing/disappearing across a filtration — genuinely close to what's needed. Adopted only partially and named explicitly in _[[11-Formal-Foundations-Survey]]_ as a conceptual borrowing (the _idea_ of tracking which clusters persist across abstraction levels `a ∈ Abs` versus which are ephemeral, matching TDA's birth/death-across-filtration language) — but full homological machinery (Betti numbers, persistence diagrams) is rejected as requiring more structure (a simplicial complex, a well-behaved metric or filtration function) than this architecture's actual, honestly-assessed proximity structure (T4) supports. Adopting the full machinery here would be exactly the "adopt because fashionable" failure mode the Seventh Task warns against.
- **Treating basins/flows as fully general dynamical systems theory (with attractors, stability analysis, Lyapunov functions).** Rejected as overreach — there is exactly one dynamical process in this architecture (Synthesize iterated on fixed `E`), and importing the full apparatus of dynamical systems theory (built for continuous or at least much richer discrete systems) would introduce machinery with no engineering referent, violating "do not invent elegant mathematics that has no engineering consequence."

## Open Questions

1. Synthesize idempotence and limit-uniqueness (T10's two hypotheses) remain the load-bearing open questions, now doubly relevant: they gate both _[[07-Transformation-Algebra]]_'s Publication-readiness predicate and this document's basin-partition theorem.
2. Whether bridge-detection and boundary-computation should be run at every Abstraction Parameter change (expensive, exact) or only opportunistically/approximately (cheaper, per the incremental-recomputation locality concerns already raised in _[[05-Design-Review-Stress-Test]]_, Section 2b/9) — a performance question, not a mathematical one, deferred to the Implementation Dependency Graph's existing recommendations (INV-30, spatial indexing).
3. Whether a formal, general definition of `θ` (the proximity threshold used throughout D21–D27) as a function of the Dimension Weighting Configuration should be fixed architecturally, or left fully to Projection-time researcher choice (an interactive "zoom" control). This document treats `θ` as a free parameter throughout, deliberately, consistent with `[[01-State-Model]]`'s classification of weighting-adjacent parameters as configuration, not `S`.
