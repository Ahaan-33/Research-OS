# Research Operating System
# Relationship Ontology

Scientific understanding emerges from relationships between Scientific Objects.

This document defines those relationships.

---

## Relationships

A relationship is a semantic connection between two or more Scientific Objects.

Relationships express meaning.

Objects express facts.

---

## Knowledge is Relational

Scientific understanding does not arise from isolated objects.

It emerges from the network formed by their relationships.

The Research State is therefore a semantic graph rather than a hierarchy.

---

## Relationships are First-Class

Relationships are part of scientific knowledge.

They should be represented explicitly rather than inferred from folders, filenames or document structure.

---

## Relationship Types

Relationship types are extensible.

Common examples include:

- supports
- contradicts
- derives from
- depends on
- measures
- observes
- explains
- implements
- supersedes
- cites
- generated by
- validated by

---

## Meaning Emerges

Scientific meaning is rarely contained within a single object.

It emerges from many connected observations, experiments, hypotheses and interpretations.

Understanding therefore increases with connectivity rather than accumulation.

---

## One Scientific Reality

There is only one Research State.

Different documents, dashboards and views do not create new knowledge.

They expose different subsets of the same semantic graph.

- [[Organisational Spaces]]

---

## Relationships Preserve Provenance

Reasoning should remain observable.

A conclusion should be traceable through the relationships that produced it.

Scientific understanding should therefore be explainable rather than opaque.
