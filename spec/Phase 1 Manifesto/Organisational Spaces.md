# Research Operating System
# Organisational Spaces

Organisational Spaces are question-specific projections of the Research State.

They organise existing knowledge without creating new knowledge.

See:
- [[Research Information Model]]
- [[Relationship Ontology]]

---

## Projection

An Organisational Space selects and arranges Scientific Objects according to a particular scientific objective.

Examples include:

- literature review
- experimental workflow
- disease mechanism
- manuscript
- project planning
- hypothesis generation

Each is a different view of the same Research State.

---

## No Duplication

Scientific Objects exist once.

An object may appear in many Organisational Spaces simultaneously without duplication.

Changes to an object are immediately reflected everywhere it appears.

---

## Purpose-Driven Organisation

Organisation answers a question.

Different questions produce different organisational spaces.

No organisation is globally correct.

---

## Concurrent Views

Many Organisational Spaces may coexist.

Each represents a valid perspective on the same underlying knowledge.

They complement rather than replace one another.

---

## Ephemeral Structure

Organisational Spaces are interfaces.

They should be inexpensive to create, modify and discard.

Scientific knowledge remains unchanged.

---

## Navigation

Organisational Spaces optimise navigation rather than storage.

They reduce cognitive effort by exposing the information required for a particular task.
